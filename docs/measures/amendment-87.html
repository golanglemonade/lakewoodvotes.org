State of Colorado · Amendment 87

87 Graduated (tiered) state income tax

A YES vote means
Creates a graduated state income tax that raises more money for K-12 education, health care, and early childhood programs, outside the TABOR revenue limit.
A NO vote means
Keeps the current constitutional requirement for one flat state income tax rate.

In plain words

Colorado now taxes all income at one flat rate of 4.4%. Starting in 2027, this would replace it with six tax brackets, from 3.7% up to 8.4%. People with taxable income up to $500,000 would pay the same or a lower rate on that income, and income above $500,000 would be taxed at 7.4% to 8.4%. The extra money would go to K-12 schools, health care, and early childhood programs, and would not count against the state's TABOR revenue limit. This measure conflicts with Proposition 136; if both pass, a court or the legislature may have to sort out the result.

Sources: Colorado Secretary of State (1, 2); Colorado Legislative Council Staff (1, 2); Colorado Newsline; Chalkbeat Colorado

Money

Estimated to raise about $958 million in its first half-year (2026-27) and about $1.97 billion in 2027-28. Legislative staff estimate taxpayers with taxable income below about $510,800 would pay less, and those above would pay more.

Who votes and what it takes to pass

Who votes: All registered voters in Colorado, including all Lakewood voters

Needs: Majority (the Blue Book says it needs a simple majority, though it changes the constitution; Colorado Newsline reports this is because its constitutional part only repeals existing language)

How it got on the ballot: Citizen initiative (petition signatures); filed as Initiative #195
