How and when to vote

Colorado votes by mail. Every registered voter gets a ballot in the mail, and you can return it by mail, at a 24-hour drop box, or vote in person.

The details

  • Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026. Ballots must be received by 7 p.m. that day. Postmarks do not count. Sources: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder (1, 2)
  • Jefferson County mails ballots and opens drop boxes on Oct. 5, 2026. Sources: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder (1, 2)
  • To get a ballot in the mail, register or update your address by Monday, Oct. 26, 2026 (8 days before Election Day). You can do this online at GoVoteColorado.gov. Sources: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder; Colorado Secretary of State
  • After Oct. 26, you can still register and vote in person at any Voter Service and Polling Center, up to and on Election Day. Source: Colorado Secretary of State
  • Monday, Oct. 26 is the last day the county advises mailing your ballot back. After that, use a drop box or a vote center. Mailed ballots need postage; drop boxes do not. Sources: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder (1, 2)
  • Drop boxes are open Oct. 5 until 7 p.m. on Nov. 3. Most are open 24 hours. Lakewood drop boxes include Belmar Library (555 S Allison Pkwy), Lakewood Civic Center (480 S Allison Pkwy), Lakewood Library (10200 W 20th Ave), Carmody, Green Mountain and Lakewood Link rec centers, Foothills Fieldhouse, Ray Ross Park, Jeffco Public Health (645 Parfet St), and drive-up boxes at Colorado Christian University and Red Rocks Community College. Full list: jeffco.us/3907/Find-a-Voting-Site. Source: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder
  • Voter Service and Polling Centers start opening Oct. 19. Hours are 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. weekdays through Nov. 2, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Saturday Oct. 31, and 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. on Election Day. Belmar Library opens Oct. 19. More Lakewood centers open Oct. 30 (Green Mountain Rec Center, Lakewood Link Rec Center, Jeffco Public Health, Red Rocks Community College, West Metro Fire Training Center) and Nov. 2 (Bear Valley Church, Lakewood Elks Lodge, Lakewood Library). Source: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder
  • You can sign up to track your ballot by text, email or phone through BallotTrax (ballottrax.coloradosos.gov). The county's Track Your Ballot link goes there. Sources: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder / Colorado Secretary of State; Colorado Secretary of State
  • You must show ID to vote in person. First-time mail voters may need to send a copy of ID with their ballot. Accepted ID includes a Colorado driver's license or ID, U.S. passport, Medicare or Medicaid card, or a utility bill, bank statement or paycheck from the last 60 days with your name and Colorado address. Digital IDs count. Source: Colorado Secretary of State
  • Jefferson County Elections: 3500 Illinois St., Suite 1100, Golden, CO 80401; phone 303-271-8111; website jeffco.us/4947/Elections. Check your registration at GoVoteColorado.gov. Sources: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder (1, 2)
  • To vote you must be a U.S. citizen, 18 or older by Election Day, and a Colorado resident for at least 22 days before the election. Source: City of Lakewood

Lakewood elections: background

  • There are no Lakewood mayor or city council races on the 2026 ballot. Lakewood elects its mayor and council in November of odd-numbered years, for four-year terms. Even years may have city ballot questions only. Source: City of Lakewood
  • The next regular Lakewood city election, with mayor and council races, is expected in November 2027 (first Tuesday of November is Nov. 2, 2027). Source: City of Lakewood
  • On April 7, 2026, Lakewood held an all-mail special election on four questions. Residents had petitioned to repeal four zoning ordinances (2025-27 to 2025-30) that council passed on Oct. 13, 2025. The new rules would have allowed duplexes, townhomes and small multiplexes in most neighborhoods that had been single-family only. A yes vote meant repeal; a no vote meant keep. Sources: City of Lakewood; Jeffco Transcript / Colorado Community Media; Denverite
  • Voters repealed all four zoning ordinances. Final official results: about 62% voted yes (repeal) and about 37% to 38% voted no on each question, with 35,760 ballots cast. Question 1, for example, was 22,310 yes to 13,357 no. The prior zoning rules came back. Sources: City of Lakewood; The Colorado Sun; Colorado Municipal League
  • The repeal side was led by Karen Gordey and the Lakewood Citizens Alliance, with Imagine Lakewood and Lakewood for All. The keep side, Make Lakewood Livable, was backed by Mayor Wendi Strom, Rep. Brittany Pettersen, the ACLU and AARP Colorado. City Council had passed a resolution urging a no vote. Sources: Denverite; The Colorado Sun; Jeffco Transcript / Colorado Community Media
  • Lakewood's measure to permanently keep revenue above TABOR limits is not on the 2026 ballot. It was Ballot Issue 2A in November 2024. The money kept is split among parks and open space, public safety, and streets and sidewalks. A 2018 vote had allowed this only through 2025. Sources: Bell Policy Center; CBS News Colorado; City of Lakewood
  • Lakewood voters approved 2024's Ballot Issue 2A, according to a local newsletter. This guide did not get the official vote count. Source: Lakewood Informer
  • The county's list of 2026 local measures shows no Jefferson County, RTD, Foothills Park & Recreation District, or library district question. For Lakewood, the local questions are 2G, 2H, 2I, 2J, 5A, 5B, 7A and 7C (7C only inside West Metro Fire). Sources: Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder (1, 2)
  • A fourth proposed charter change, on how to fill council vacancies, did not pass council (0-11 on Aug. 10, 2026) and is not on the ballot. Sources: City of Lakewood (1, 2)
