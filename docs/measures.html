State of Colorado

14 measures

How statewide measures work

  • Thirteen of the 14 measures were put on the ballot by citizen petitions. Only Proposition NN was sent to voters by the state legislature. Sources: Colorado Legislative Council Staff; Ballotpedia News
  • An 'Amendment' changes the Colorado Constitution and usually needs 55% of the vote to pass. A 'Proposition' changes state law and needs a simple majority. Sources: Colorado Legislative Council Staff; Ballotpedia News

81 Police must tell federal immigration officials about certain charges

Amendment 81 · Needs: 55% (constitutional amendment)

Yes means
Law enforcement must notify federal immigration authorities after filing certain criminal charges against people who are not confirmed to be lawfully present.
No means
Current law stays the same, which allows some cooperation in criminal matters but limits local involvement in civil immigration enforcement.

82 Right to buy and sell natural gas

Amendment 82 · Needs: 55% (constitutional amendment)

Yes means
Creates a state constitutional right to buy and sell natural gas for use in homes and businesses.
No means
Current laws and rules about buying and selling natural gas stay the same.

83 Right to hunt and fish

Amendment 83 · Needs: 55% (constitutional amendment)

Yes means
Creates a state constitutional right to hunt and fish.
No means
Hunting and fishing continue as allowed under current state and federal law.

84 ID number required on mail ballots

Amendment 84 · Needs: 55% (constitutional amendment)

Yes means
Voters must provide identification information with their mail ballot, in addition to their signature.
No means
Mail ballots continue to be checked by signature only.

85 Plain-language ballot titles

Amendment 85 · Needs: 55% (constitutional amendment)

Yes means
Ballot titles must be written in plain language at an eighth-grade reading level.
No means
Current law stays; titles must still be clear, but there is no set reading-level standard.

86 Rules for redrawing congressional districts between censuses

Amendment 86 · Needs: 55% (constitutional amendment)

Yes means
Any new congressional map drawn outside the usual once-a-decade schedule must be approved by the independent commission and the state Supreme Court.
No means
The current system stays, in which congressional districts are redrawn every ten years.

87 Graduated (tiered) state income tax

Amendment 87 · Needs: Majority (the Blue Book says it needs a simple majority, though it changes the constitution; Colorado Newsline reports this is because its constitutional part only repeals existing language)

Yes means
Creates a graduated state income tax that raises more money for K-12 education, health care, and early childhood programs, outside the TABOR revenue limit.
No means
Keeps the current constitutional requirement for one flat state income tax rate.

NN Let the state keep TABOR refund money for schools and kids

Proposition NN · Needs: Majority

Yes means
The state can keep some or all money that would otherwise be refunded under TABOR and spend it on K-12 education, children's programs, and other purposes.
No means
TABOR refunds continue as under current law, and current funding levels stay the same.

132 Tougher penalties for fentanyl crimes

Proposition 132 · Needs: Majority

Yes means
Possessing any amount of fentanyl becomes a felony, distribution carries a mandatory minimum of 8 years in prison, and some options to reduce penalties end.
No means
Possessing a small amount stays a misdemeanor, distribution penalties stay based on quantity, and current options to reduce penalties remain.

133 Life in prison for child sex trafficking

Proposition 133 · Needs: Majority

Yes means
The penalty for child sex trafficking becomes life in prison without parole, and the crime is expanded to include buying sexual activity with a minor.
No means
Current penalties of 8 to 48 years in prison plus mandatory parole stay in place.

134 School and college sports teams based on biological sex

Proposition 134 · Needs: Majority

Yes means
All school-based teams must be designated for biological males, biological females, or mixed, and schools must adopt sex-eligibility policies.
No means
Schools, districts, and athletic associations keep setting their own policies under current state and federal law.

135 Ban on gender-related surgery for minors

Proposition 135 · Needs: Majority

Yes means
These surgeries on minors are prohibited, and public funds and insurance cannot pay for them.
No means
Current state law stays, which protects access to these surgeries and allows insurance to cover them.

136 Cap the state income tax rate at 4.4%

Proposition 136 · Needs: Majority

Yes means
The current 4.4% rate becomes the highest rate allowed in state law.
No means
The rate stays at 4.4% for now, but no new limit is written into law.

137 Use sporting goods sales tax for conservation and wildfire work

Proposition 137 · Needs: Majority

Yes means
Sporting goods sales tax money goes to the named conservation and wildfire programs and is exempt from the state revenue limit.
No means
That money stays in the state's general budget, under the revenue limit, to be spent by the legislature or refunded when required.

City of Lakewood

4 measures

2G City sales tax increase for public safety and traffic safety

Ballot Issue 2G · Needs: Majority

Yes means
The city sales tax goes up by half a cent per dollar (to 3.5%), with the money set aside for public safety and traffic safety.
No means
The city sales tax stays at 3%.

2H When new mayor and council terms begin

Ballot Question 2H · Needs: Majority

Yes means
New mayor and council terms start at the first regular council meeting after results are certified.
No means
The charter's current wording on when terms start stays the same.

2I Voter approval required for city property tax increases

Ballot Question 2I · Needs: Majority

Yes means
The charter's property tax section is replaced with language that bars city property tax increases without a vote of the people.
No means
The current Section 12.12 of the charter stays as it is.

2J Timing of elections on citizen initiatives and referendums

Ballot Question 2J · Needs: Majority

Yes means
Elections on citizen initiatives and referendums would be held 60 to 150 days after a petition is found sufficient.
No means
The current 30-to-90-day window stays in place.

Jeffco Public Schools (Jefferson County School District R-1)

2 measures

5A School property tax for staff pay and career programs

Ballot Issue 5A · Needs: Majority

Yes means
Property taxes go up to give Jeffco Schools $73 million more per year for staff pay and career programs.
No means
This school property tax increase does not happen.

5B School property tax for building repairs, technology and safety

Ballot Issue 5B · Needs: Majority

Yes means
Property taxes go up to give Jeffco Schools $60 million a year, rising with inflation, for building repairs, technology and safety.
No means
This school property tax increase does not happen.

West Metro Fire Protection District

1 measure

7C Fire district sales tax for emergency medical and fire services

Ballot Issue 7C · Needs: Majority

Yes means
A new 0.75% fire district sales tax is added (75 cents on a $100 purchase) to fund fire and emergency medical services.
No means
No new fire district sales tax.

Front Range Passenger Rail District

1 measure

7A Sales tax and borrowing for Front Range passenger rail

Ballot Issue 7A · Needs: Majority of all votes cast across the whole district

Yes means
A new 0.333% district sales tax is added and the district may borrow up to $580 million for Front Range passenger rail.
No means
No new rail district sales tax or borrowing.
